NCGC Secures N46.95B Loans via N21.59B Guarantees, Says Tinubu

Published 2026-09-28 · Auto-generated from Nigerian financial news

The Nigerian economy just got a massive boost: the National Credit Guarantee Company (NCGC) has unlocked ₦46.95 billion in loans in its first year of operation, backed by ₦21.59 billion in government guarantees. That’s roughly the equivalent of financing more than 67,500 borrowers across 25 states and the Federal Capital Territory – many of them accessing formal credit for the very first time. For the average Nigerian, this translates into a flood of new lending opportunities, lower borrowing costs and a clearer path to turning big‑ticket dreams into reality.

Why the NCGC breakthrough matters to you

#### 1. More credit, cheaper rates

The NCGC’s guarantee scheme reduces the risk that banks face when lending to individuals and small‑to‑medium enterprises (SMEs). With the government covering up to 80 % of a borrower’s default risk, banks can afford to lower interest rates and relax collateral requirements. For a typical personal loan of ₦2 million, a 10 % reduction in the annual percentage rate (APR) could shave off ₦200,000 in interest over a five‑year term. Premium‑Times reports that about a third of the ₦46.95 billion in loans went to people who had never accessed formal credit before. This is a game‑changer for the informal‑sector worker who has been relying on costly money‑lenders. With a formal loan, you can finance a small shop, buy a motorbike for delivery work, or invest in a modest solar power system to cut electricity bills. #### 3. Spill‑over effects on the wider economy

When more Nigerians can borrow at affordable rates, consumption and investment rise. A farmer who secures a ₦5 million loan can buy a tractor, increase yields, and sell more produce at market. A young professional who obtains a ₦3 million loan can finally enrol in a professional certification programme, boosting earning potential. The aggregate effect is higher tax revenues, more jobs and a stronger Naira.

Supporting story: CBN’s policy buffers keep the Naira‑Dollar outlook stable

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has maintained sizeable foreign‑exchange reserves, acting as a buffer against volatile global markets. This stability is crucial for borrowers because it dampens sudden spikes in the Naira‑Dollar exchange rate, which would otherwise push up the cost of imported goods and raise the price of imported loan‑linked assets (e.g., cars, construction materials, solar panels).

For a Nigerian who finances a ₦8 million auto purchase, a 5 % depreciation of the Naira could increase the effective cost of the vehicle by ₦400,000 if the loan is denominated in dollars. The CBN’s reserve cushion helps keep such currency shocks in check, protecting both borrowers and lenders from unexpected cost hikes.
